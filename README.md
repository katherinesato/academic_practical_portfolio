### 📄 Foundational Academic Papers

* [x] **On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the Entscheidungsproblem** — *Alan Turing (1936)*
  > **Why it matters:** The paper where computing was born. Defines the Universal Turing Machine and the foundational concept of algorithms.
* [x] **A Symbolic Analysis of Relay and Switching Circuits** — *Claude Shannon (1937)*
  > **Why it matters:** Widely considered one of the most important master's theses of the 20th century. Proved that Boolean Algebra (TRUE/FALSE logic) could be directly implemented using electrical circuits with switches and relays—forming the foundation of all modern computer hardware.
* [x] **First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC** — *John von Neumann (1945)*
  > **Why it matters:** The blueprint for the Von Neumann Architecture. Transformed Turing's theoretical machine into the structural design of modern computers.
* [x] **A Mathematical Theory of Communication** — *Claude Shannon (1948)*
  > **Why it matters:** Founded Information Theory. Proved that all information (audio, image, text, video) can be translated and reliably transmitted through fundamental units called bits.
* [x] **On Certain Formal Properties of Grammars** — *Noam Chomsky (1959)*
  > **Why it matters:** Defined the hierarchy of formal languages, laying the groundwork for modern compilers and programming language design.
* [x] **Architecture of the IBM System/360** — *Gene Amdahl, Gerrit Blaauw & Fred Brooks (1964)*
  > **Why it matters:** Outlines key hardware design decisions that persist today—including the standard adoption of Two's Complement for integers and defining 1 Byte as exactly 8 bits.
